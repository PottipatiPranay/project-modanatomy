The Electrical Signature

As your model's piston pumps, it's performing the same mechanical work that the heart's electrical system coordinates. This EKG waveform shows what that electrical signal looks like in a real heart.

Each heartbeat follows the same pattern: atrial activation (P wave), ventricular activation (QRS complex), and ventricular recovery (T wave).

How it works

The mechanics, step by step

01

Pull stroke — Diastole

Pulling the lever arm retracts the piston, creating a vacuum inside the chamber. Suction draws colored water in through the inlet valve — a mechanical analog for how the heart's chambers fill with blood during diastole.

02

Push stroke — Systole

Pressing the lever arm drives the piston forward, building pressure. The outlet valve opens and water is ejected into the tubing — analogous to the heart's systolic contraction.

03

Valve action — Unidirectional flow

Silicone valves prevent backflow at both inlet and outlet. Students can observe that colored water only travels in one direction — the same principle behind the heart's mitral, aortic, tricuspid, and pulmonary valves.

04

The key engineering challenge — Piston sealing

Creating a reliable vacuum with a piston required several iterations. The solution: custom rubber gaskets cut to the piston face inlaid in a groove in the piston's side. The result is a compressible, waterproof gasket that seals under negative pressure.
